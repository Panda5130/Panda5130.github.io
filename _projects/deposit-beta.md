## Question

A bank's **deposit beta** is the long-run share of a change in the policy rate that it passes through to the rate it pays on deposits. It drives net interest income and economic value of equity sensitivity, deposit pricing and funds-transfer pricing. This project estimates it bank by bank for a peer group of seven regional banks from public regulatory data: First Horizon, Glacier, Huntington, KeyBank, M&T, Old National and SouthState.

## Data

- **Deposit rate** *y*: 400 × quarterly deposit interest expense ÷ the average of current and prior quarter-end total deposits, from FDIC Call Reports (BankFind Suite API).
- **Policy rate** *x*: quarterly average of the effective federal funds rate (FRED `FEDFUNDS`).
- **Mergers**: every acquisition of at least 1% of the survivor's deposits since 2007 (70 events in the sample) is backfilled pro forma, so each series reflects today's combined franchise rather than jumps at deal dates.

## Model

Both series are I(1) for six of the seven banks (ADF and KPSS agree; Glacier is borderline), so the relationship is estimated as a single-equation error-correction model (ECM):

<p class="eq">Δy<sub>t</sub> = λ (y<sub>t−1</sub> − c − θ x<sub>t−1</sub>) + Σ<sub>i</sub> γ<sub>i</sub> Δx<sub>t−i</sub> + Σ<sub>j</sub> φ<sub>j</sub> Δy<sub>t−j</sub> + ε<sub>t</sub></p>

θ is the long-run deposit beta and λ < 0 the speed of adjustment: the share of any gap from equilibrium that closes each quarter. For each bank:

1. Test cointegration over a grid of Engle-Granger, Johansen trace and ARDL bounds-test specifications (deterministic terms, lag criteria).
2. Keep specifications that cointegrate at 5%, map each to a one-step ECM, and prune short-run lags with p ≥ 0.05.
3. Among those, take the specification with the lowest in-sample error as the champion.

The champion then has to pass the selection and validation criteria further down: beta stability, out-of-sample forecasts, a structural-break test and seven challenger models.

## Results: long-run pass-through

Every bank cointegrates with the funds rate in at least one specification, and every champion's λ and θ are significant at 5%. Long-run betas fall in a narrow band, 0.35 to 0.52.

## Economic reading

- **Pass-through.** At the peer median, a sustained 100 bp move in the funds rate moves the average deposit rate by about 43 bp in the long run. The remainder is the deposit franchise's margin.
- **Glacier is the outlier** (θ = 0.35): its rate stayed well below peers through both hiking cycles, consistent with a lower-cost deposit base. Its beta is also the least stable: estimated on data before the 2022–23 hikes, it was only 0.11 to 0.14 (Figure 3).
- **Speed and short-run dynamics.** First Horizon closes 37% of any gap from equilibrium each quarter (half-life 1.5 quarters). Huntington, Old National and SouthState have λ below 0.09 in magnitude (half-lives of 8 to 10 quarters) but pass 14% to 18% of a funds-rate change through in the same quarter. For them, near-term repricing runs mainly through the short-run terms rather than through error correction.
- **Cycle betas are rising.** Realized pass-through over each rate cycle (Δ deposit rate ÷ Δ funds rate, between funds-rate turning points) had a median of 0.23 in the 2015–19 hikes and 0.40 in the 2022–24 hikes. The second cycle was larger and faster, and pass-through was higher at every bank.
- **Asymmetry.** The cuts since 2024 have passed through 0.34 so far, against 0.40 on the way up. Six of seven banks adjust downward more slowly than upward, but the difference is significant (at 10%) only for SouthState. An asymmetric ECM does not improve forecasts across the peer group.

## Selection and validation criteria

A champion is judged on four criteria. Long-run beta stability comes first, because θ is the quantity the project exists to estimate. Forecast accuracy is one criterion among four, not the deciding one.

1. **Statistical.** The specification cointegrates with the funds rate at 5%, λ is negative, and λ and θ are both significant at 5%. All seven champions pass.
2. **Long-run beta stability.** Each champion is re-estimated at every quarter from 2016Q1 on the data available at that date, and its θ is compared with the full-sample estimate. A start date fails when θ is both more than 2 standard errors and more than 20% away. Requiring both counts only moves that are statistically and economically large. Only start dates from 2020Q1 are rated, because earlier samples are mostly at the zero lower bound, where θ is poorly identified. Green means no failing start dates, Amber one or two, and Red three or more.
3. **Out-of-sample forecasts.** The champion is re-estimated at every quarter from 2016Q1 and forecast up to 8 quarters ahead along the realized funds-rate path. It is rated against error limits derived from the peer group and against a no-change forecast.
4. **Challengers and breaks.** The champion must not be beaten across the peer group by simpler or alternative model forms, and its cointegration result must survive a structural-break test.

### Challengers and structural breaks

- **Challengers.** None of seven challengers beats the champions across the peer group. They are an asymmetric ECM (two forms), static and cycle-specific OLS betas, and the champions with a 2022 dummy. Static and cycle betas have no lag structure and overshoot during hikes.
- **Structural break.** Every series has a 2022 break, and so does the funds rate. Gregory-Hansen tests allow one unknown break in the cointegrating relation, with critical values simulated for 62 quarters. They do not reject for any bank, so the common break cancels in the relationship. A 2022Q3 level dummy worsens out-of-sample forecasts for six of seven banks.

## Limitations

- 62 quarterly observations limit the power of unit-root and cointegration tests; results are not robust to starting the sample in 2008.
- The funds rate is the only driver: there are no competitive, liquidity or balance-sheet variables.
- Huntington's cointegration rests on one specification family and on post-2022 data. It is kept in scope with that caveat.
- Forecast limits are relative to the peer group, so one bank's re-specification can change another's rating.
- Beta stability is rated only from 2020Q1. Glacier's and M&T's long-run betas are not stable over that window.
- Champions are chosen on in-sample error. Choosing on out-of-sample error instead would change six of seven models, with mixed effects. That choice is left to validation.
- The beta is on total deposits, not interest-bearing deposits or individual products.
- Short-run terms are screened on p-values, so their reported significance is optimistic.
