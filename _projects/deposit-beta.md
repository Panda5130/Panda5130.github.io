## Question

A bank's **deposit beta** is the long-run share of a change in the policy rate that it passes through to the rate it pays on deposits. It drives net interest income and economic value of equity sensitivity, deposit pricing and funds-transfer pricing. This project estimates it bank by bank for a peer group of seven regional banks from public regulatory data: First Horizon, Glacier, Huntington, KeyBank, M&T, Old National and SouthState.

## Data

- **Deposit rate** *y*: 400 × quarterly deposit interest expense ÷ the average of current and prior quarter-end total deposits, from FDIC Call Reports (BankFind Suite API).
- **Policy rate** *x*: quarterly average of the effective federal funds rate (FRED `FEDFUNDS`).
- **Mergers**: every acquisition of at least 1% of the survivor's deposits since 2007 (70 events in the sample) is backfilled pro forma, so each series reflects today's combined franchise rather than jumps at deal dates.

## Model

Both series are I(1) for six of the seven banks (ADF and KPSS agree; Glacier is borderline), so the relationship is estimated as a single-equation error-correction model (ECM):

<p class="eq">Δy<sub>t</sub> = λ (y<sub>t−1</sub> − c − θ x<sub>t−1</sub>) + Σ<sub>i</sub> γ<sub>i</sub> Δx<sub>t−i</sub> + Σ<sub>j</sub> φ<sub>j</sub> Δy<sub>t−j</sub> + ε<sub>t</sub></p>

θ is the long-run deposit beta and λ < 0 the speed of adjustment: the share of any gap from equilibrium that closes each quarter. For each bank:

1. Test cointegration over a grid of Engle-Granger, Johansen trace and ARDL bounds-test specifications (deterministic terms, lag criteria).
2. Keep specifications that cointegrate at 5% and map each to a one-step ECM. Prune short-run lags with p ≥ 0.05, then an insignificant trend and constant.
3. Give each surviving specification one asymmetric variant. Its speed of adjustment depends on whether the deposit rate is above or below equilibrium, its short-run terms are split by the sign of the change, and the long-run beta may differ for rises and cuts (equation below). Split terms collapse back to one when a Wald test does not reject equality at 5%.
4. Choose the champion out of sample. Rank every admissible candidate (λ in (−1, 0), θ in [0, 1]) by its mean forecast error from 1 to 8 quarters ahead, and take the one with the fewest parameters within one standard error of the best.

The asymmetric variant, with ect<sup>+</sup> and ect<sup>−</sup> the positive and negative parts of the equilibrium error:

<p class="eq">Δy<sub>t</sub> = λ<sup>+</sup> ect<sup>+</sup><sub>t−1</sub> + λ<sup>−</sup> ect<sup>−</sup><sub>t−1</sub> + Σ<sub>i</sub> (γ<sup>+</sup><sub>i</sub> Δx<sup>+</sup><sub>t−i</sub> + γ<sup>−</sup><sub>i</sub> Δx<sup>−</sup><sub>t−i</sub>) + Σ<sub>j</sub> (φ<sup>+</sup><sub>j</sub> Δy<sup>+</sup><sub>t−j</sub> + φ<sup>−</sup><sub>j</sub> Δy<sup>−</sup><sub>t−j</sub>) + ε<sub>t</sub></p>

The champion is then checked against the criteria further down: out-of-sample forecasts, a structural-break test and seven challenger models. Long-run beta stability is reported alongside, to compare the banks.

## Results: long-run pass-through

Every bank cointegrates with the funds rate in at least one specification, and every champion's λ and θ are significant at 5%. Six of the seven champions changed when selection moved from in-sample to out-of-sample error; only Huntington's is the same. Long-run betas run from 0.23 (Glacier) to 0.52 (SouthState), and the other five lie between 0.40 and 0.48.

## Economic reading

- **Pass-through.** At the peer median, a sustained 100 bp move in the funds rate moves the average deposit rate by about 42 bp in the long run. The remainder is the deposit franchise's margin.
- **Glacier is the outlier** (θ = 0.23): its rate stayed well below peers through both hiking cycles, consistent with a lower-cost deposit base. Its beta is also the least stable: estimated on data before the 2022–23 hikes, it was only 0.03 to 0.08 (Figure 4).
- **Speed and short-run dynamics.** First Horizon closes 23% of any gap from equilibrium each quarter (half-life 2.7 quarters), M&T 19% (3.2 quarters). Huntington is the slowest (λ = −0.07, half-life 10 quarters) but passes 17% of a funds-rate change through in the same quarter, so its near-term repricing runs mainly through the short-run terms rather than through error correction. SouthState and M&T also pass 17% to 20% through on impact.
- **Cycle betas are rising.** Realized pass-through over each rate cycle (Δ deposit rate ÷ Δ funds rate, between funds-rate turning points) had a median of 0.23 in the 2015–19 hikes and 0.40 in the 2022–24 hikes. The second cycle was larger and faster, and pass-through was higher at every bank.
- **Asymmetry.** The cuts since 2024 have passed through 0.34 so far, against 0.40 on the way up. Across the 78 asymmetric variants, equal adjustment speeds above and below equilibrium are rejected at 5% in 54 and an equal long-run beta in 31: asymmetric adjustment is common, an asymmetric long-run beta less so. Only KeyBank's champion is asymmetric. It closes a gap about 3.7 times faster when its deposit rate is below equilibrium than above (λ<sup>−</sup> = −0.33 against λ<sup>+</sup> = −0.09), and it passes 26% of a cut through in the same quarter against an insignificant 3% of a rise. Its long-run beta is the same in both directions.

## Validation and peer evaluation

Out-of-sample forecasts validate each bank's model: they choose the champion and give it its forecast rating. Long-run beta stability evaluates the peer group: it shows how far each bank's estimated beta can be relied on when the banks are compared.

1. **Statistical.** The specification cointegrates with the funds rate at 5%, λ is negative, and λ and θ are both significant at 5%. All seven champions pass.
2. **Out-of-sample forecasts (model validation).** The champion is re-estimated at every quarter from 2016Q1 on the data available at that date and forecast up to 8 quarters ahead along the realized funds-rate path. It is rated against fixed error limits by horizon band, frozen from an approved reference run on 11 November 2026, and against a no-change forecast: Red if it does no better than no-change at any horizon.
3. **Challengers and breaks (model validation).** The champion is compared with simpler or alternative model forms, and its cointegration result must survive a structural-break test.
4. **Long-run beta stability (peer evaluation).** Each champion is re-estimated at every quarter from 2016Q1, and its θ is compared with the full-sample estimate. A start date fails when θ is both more than 2 standard errors and more than 20% away. Requiring both counts only moves that are statistically and economically large. Only start dates from 2020Q1 are rated, because earlier samples are mostly at the zero lower bound, where θ is poorly identified. Green means no failing start dates, Amber one or two, and Red three or more.

### Challengers and structural breaks

- **Challengers.** Seven challengers are scored on the same start dates: an asymmetric ECM (two forms), static and cycle-specific OLS betas, and the champions with a 2022 dummy. Because the parsimony rule picks simpler champions, some challengers now score slightly lower: the asymmetric-λ ECM at five of seven banks (peer mean 0.233 against 0.234 pp), and cycle betas at Glacier (0.20 against 0.27 pp). Challengers are reference results, not candidates, so they do not replace champions. Static and cycle betas have no lag structure and overshoot during hikes.
- **Structural break.** Every series has a 2022 break, and so does the funds rate. Gregory-Hansen tests allow one unknown break in the cointegrating relation, with critical values simulated for 62 quarters. They do not reject for any bank, so the common break cancels in the relationship. A 2022Q3 level dummy worsens out-of-sample forecasts at every bank.

## Limitations

- 62 quarterly observations limit the power of unit-root and cointegration tests; results are not robust to starting the sample in 2008.
- The funds rate is the only driver: there are no competitive, liquidity or balance-sheet variables.
- Huntington's cointegration rests on one specification family and on post-2022 data. It is kept in scope with that caveat.
- Forecast limits are frozen from the November 2026 champions as a peer group. They no longer move when one bank is re-specified, but they need a governed re-freeze when the data or champions change.
- Beta stability is rated only from 2020Q1. Glacier's, KeyBank's, M&T's and SouthState's long-run betas are not stable over that window.
- Champions are chosen on the same out-of-sample backtest that rates them, which makes their forecast ratings somewhat optimistic. The one-standard-error rule gives up some accuracy for fewer parameters at four banks.
- Insignificant trends and constants are pruned after the cointegration screen without re-testing, so Glacier's and M&T's fitted forms differ from the forms under which they cointegrate.
- The asymmetric fits are fragile: 23 of 78 cycled between regime splits, and some admissible variants forecast explosively. KeyBank's λ split rests on the regimes of one sample.
- The beta is on total deposits, not interest-bearing deposits or individual products.
- Short-run terms are screened on p-values, so their reported significance is optimistic.
